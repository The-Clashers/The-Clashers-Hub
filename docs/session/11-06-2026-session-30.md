## Decisions

- Stars are the API's truth, never derived from the fill. The live payload
  proves they are independent: "Sharing is caring" holds 2 stars at 25% of
  the final target, "Dragon Slayer" holds 1 star with the value eight
  times past it, and 35 of 54 rows are 3-star with value under or past
  target in every combination. The game earns stars at in-game thresholds
  the payload does not expose, so any derivation would disagree with the
  player's own profile. The rail therefore carries the fraction (capped at
  1) and the stars separately: the fill says how far the counter has
  climbed, the markers say how many stars that climb has already earned.
- The rail caps its fill at 1 but the pair never lies: a row past its
  target ("Gold Grab", 2,000,000,000 / 100,000,000) renders the real
  numbers beside a full rail. The pair is the record; the rail is the
  shape.
- Completed rows mute instead of disappearing. The card is a record first
  (what the player has earned) and a to-do second (what is left), and a
  maxed account still deserves its 54 rows. The ledger treatment keeps
  the record while letting the in-progress rows carry the visual weight;
  on Yeon's real payload the five 0- and 1- and 2-star rows read instantly
  against the muted 35.
- The value/target pair formats with thousands separators
  ("2,000,000,000 / 100,000,000"): the game renders its own counters that
  way, and the design language allows no decimal noise (07 section 5).
  formatProgressPair lives in the lib module so the pair and the rail can
  never disagree about a number.
- The empty array stays undefined rather than parsing to an empty list:
  the same convention the unit arrays keep (a payload that omits the array
  and one that sends it empty mean the same thing to the screen), and the
  card renders one quiet notice for both, never a zero-row card.
- No separate loading/error states for the card: the fetch is the shell's
  own. The card is derived from the response the shell already holds
  (SR-15), so it inherits the shell's state matrix for free; the skeleton
  already draws a section shape where the card lands.
