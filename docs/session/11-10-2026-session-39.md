## Decisions

- Blur on the big surfaces only. Backdrop blur is the expensive property in
  the system, so the base panels, the nav bar, and the floating chrome
  (listbox, hint) take the shared filter, while pills, chips, inputs, and
  tiles paint with the translucent token colors alone: per-panel blur reads
  as glass, per-control blur reads as lag.
- Contrast is checked against the worst-case panel blend, not the flat hex:
  a translucent panel's real background is the panel composited over
  whatever sits behind it, and the strongest thing that can sit behind any
  panel is the canvas carrying a full glow blob. Under that method the
  lowest text pair is textMuted on the blend at 5.8:1 (light) and 7.0:1
  (dark), both clear of the 4.5:1 floor, and the doc now records the
  method with the numbers.
- The refresh control moved into the player card's actions column (the
  owner's placement call): freshness belongs to the card whose data it
  describes, beside the compare action. The clan screen keeps its footer
  row until its own pass; the control is the same component either way.
- The compare button routes to /compare as it stands: the compare screen
  is a placeholder and its sub-routes land with the compare cards (the
  App.tsx comment already says so), so deep-linking a tag no screen reads
  yet would be dead weight. The button's hint says exactly that for screen
  readers.
- The gauge is built on react-native-svg rather than a View-trick arc: the
  concept stack already blesses the library for charts, the prototype's
  dial is an SVG path with a dash-array fill, and partial arcs from border
  tricks are fragile at arbitrary fractions.
- The band vocabulary is shared, not duplicated: the card maps each
  category percentage through the engine's own `bandFor` and only then to
  a color, so a bar can never disagree with the chip about where a
  reading falls.
- Glow fields live behind every screen as one fixed layer, not per-screen
  decoration: they never scroll, never intercept a pointer, and the panels
  blur them, which is what makes the glass read as lit rather than fogged.
